> Rimal Byte is a fictional company. This policy was written for a portfolio GRC project.

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Rimal Byte finds, rates, treats and records cybersecurity risks to the
confidentiality, integrity and availability of its information and technology. The General
Manager approves it as the company's risk management method. [1-5-1]

## Scope

Every cybersecurity risk to company laptops, Microsoft 365, the office network, client data and
the software projects Rimal Byte delivers.

## Policy

1. The Cybersecurity Lead must keep a risk register and apply this method to every risk in it.
   [1-5-2]
2. Each risk must be rated for likelihood, using this scale of how often the event is
   expected. [1-5-1]

   | Level | Name | Meaning |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | Rare | Not expected within five years |
   | 2 | Unlikely | Expected about once in three to five years |
   | 3 | Possible | Expected about once in one to three years |
   | 4 | Likely | Expected about once a year |
   | 5 | Almost certain | Expected several times a year, or already happening |

3. Each risk must be rated for impact on the company or its clients, using this scale. [1-5-1]

   | Level | Name | Meaning |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | Minimal | Fixed within a day by normal work; no lasting effect |
   | 2 | Minor | One person or service affected for up to a few days; no client data exposed |
   | 3 | Moderate | Several staff or one client affected for up to a week |
   | 4 | Major | Client data exposed or several clients affected; recovery takes weeks |
   | 5 | Severe | Serious breach of client data, loss of a government contract, or the company cannot operate |

4. The score must be likelihood x impact, and the rating must come from the score. [1-5-1]

   | Score | Rating |
   |---|---|
   | 1-4 | Low |
   | 5-9 | Medium |
   | 10-16 | High |
   | 20-25 | Critical |

   No likelihood and impact pair gives a score of 17 to 19 or 21 to 24, so these bands cover
   every possible score.
5. Each risk must be given one treatment: mitigate (reduce it with controls), accept (live with
   it), transfer (move it to another party, for example through a contract or insurance) or avoid
   (stop the activity that causes it). [1-5-1, 1-5-2]
6. Low and Medium risks must be accepted by the Cybersecurity Lead or the General Manager.
   High and Critical risks must be accepted only by the General Manager. [1-5-1, 1-5-2]
7. The register must record, for each risk: its id, the linked ECC controls, the risk statement,
   likelihood, impact, treatment, owner and due date. The owner is a role, and the due date
   decides how soon the risk appears on the roadmap. A risk is closed when every linked ECC
   control is implemented or not applicable. [1-5-2]
8. A risk assessment must be carried out: [1-5-3]
   - at the early stage of a technology project [1.5.3.1]
   - before a major change to technology infrastructure [1.5.3.2]
   - when planning to use a third-party service [1.5.3.3]
   - before a new technology service or product is released [1.5.3.4]
9. The Cybersecurity Lead must carry out a full assessment against ECC every year and report the
   results to the supervisory committee. [1-5-2, 1-8-1]

## Roles

- **General Manager:** approves this method and accepts High and Critical risks.
- **Cybersecurity Lead:** runs the method, keeps the register and accepts Low and Medium risks.
- **IT Manager and Project Manager:** report changes and projects that need an assessment, and
  own the risks assigned to them.
- **Staff:** tell the Cybersecurity Lead about any risk they notice.

## Exceptions

An exception to any policy must be requested from the Cybersecurity Lead in writing. The
Cybersecurity Lead assesses it with this method and the General Manager approves or refuses it,
whatever its rating. An approved exception has an owner and an end date no more than 12 months
away, and is recorded in the risk register.

## Compliance

A breach of this policy is handled under the HR disciplinary rules. Anyone who suspects a breach
must report it to the Cybersecurity Lead.

## Review

The Cybersecurity Lead reviews this method every year in October, or sooner if the law or the
business changes significantly. Changes are recorded and approved by the General Manager. [1-5-4]
