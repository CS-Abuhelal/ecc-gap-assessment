> Rimal Byte is a fictional company. This policy was written for a portfolio GRC project.

## Purpose

Rimal Byte is an IT services company in Jeddah with about 30 staff. Clients trust it with their
systems and data, and it is bidding for government contracts. This policy is the top-level
statement of how the company protects information and technology. It commits the company to a
cybersecurity program aligned with the National Cybersecurity Authority's Essential Cybersecurity
Controls (ECC-2:2024).

## Scope

This policy applies to all staff and contractors, and to company laptops, Microsoft 365, the
office network and client data.

## Policy

1. The General Manager, as Authorized Official, commits Rimal Byte to a cybersecurity program
   aligned with ECC-2:2024 and must approve every cybersecurity policy and procedure before it
   takes effect. [1-3-1]
2. The Cybersecurity Lead must write down the company's cybersecurity policies and procedures,
   including the controls and requirements in them. [1-3-1]
3. The detailed requirements are in five topic policies. They are part of this policy, and where
   they differ from it the stricter requirement applies. [1-3-1]
   - Roles and Responsibilities (`roles-and-responsibilities.md`)
   - Risk Management Method (`risk-management-method.md`)
   - Access Control Policy (`access-control-policy.md`)
   - Vulnerability Management Policy (`vulnerability-management-policy.md`)
   - Logging and Monitoring Policy (`logging-and-monitoring-policy.md`)
4. The CIS Microsoft Windows 11 Enterprise Benchmark v3.0.0 (Level 1) is the security baseline
   for every company laptop. The IT Manager must apply it when a laptop is set up and keep it
   applied. [1-3-3, 2-3-1]
5. Every deviation from the baseline must be recorded as an exception. A laptop that runs
   Windows 11 Home cannot apply every setting, so until it moves to Windows 11 Pro or
   Enterprise each setting it cannot meet is an exception. [1-3-3, 2-3-1]
6. The Cybersecurity Lead must publish the current version of every policy where all staff can
   read it, and must tell staff when one changes. [1-3-1]
7. Staff and contractors must read and acknowledge these policies when they join and again after
   each update, and must follow them. The HR Officer keeps the acknowledgments. [1-3-1, 1-9-4]
8. The Cybersecurity Lead must check that the policies are followed, and must report the results,
   breaches and open exceptions to the supervisory committee every quarter. [1-3-2]
9. Every change to a policy must be recorded as a new version number and approved by the
   General Manager. [1-3-4]

## Roles

- **General Manager:** approves the policies and gives the program the people and time it needs.
- **Cybersecurity Lead:** owns the policies, checks that they are followed and reports to the
  supervisory committee.
- **IT Manager:** applies the Windows baseline on every laptop and records deviations.
- **HR Officer:** gives new staff the policies, keeps the acknowledgments and applies the HR
  disciplinary rules.
- **Project Manager:** makes sure contractors on a client project have acknowledged the policies
  before they get access.
- **Staff:** read, acknowledge and follow the policies, and report any breach they see.

## Exceptions

An exception must be requested from the Cybersecurity Lead in writing. The Cybersecurity Lead
assesses it with the risk management method and the General Manager approves or refuses it. An
approved exception has an owner and an end date no more than 12 months away, and is recorded in
the risk register.

## Compliance

A breach of this policy is handled under the HR disciplinary rules. Anyone who suspects a breach
must report it to the Cybersecurity Lead.

## Review

The Cybersecurity Lead reviews this policy every year in October, or sooner if the law or the
business changes significantly. The review covers the five topic policies and the
baseline. [1-3-4]
